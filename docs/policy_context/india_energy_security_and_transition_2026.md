# India Energy Security and Transition Context — 2026 Evidence Note

This note records authoritative public evidence that motivates URJA-2047. Values are time-stamped because energy statistics change rapidly.

## 1. Petroleum and gas import exposure

The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas reported through the Press Information Bureau on 29 October 2025 that India was meeting **about 88% of crude-oil needs and 51% of gas needs through imports**. The same statement noted crude-oil consumption around 5.6 million barrels per day and reported that India imported around 300 million metric tonnes of crude and petroleum products during 2024–25. This import exposure gives the transition a direct energy-security and foreign-exchange dimension in addition to climate mitigation.

The strategic risk is not theoretical. During the 2026 West Asia supply disruption, Government briefings described active diversification of crude routes and suppliers, prioritised natural-gas allocation and emergency monitoring of energy supplies. URJA-2047 therefore treats energy security and climate transition as linked spatial-planning problems.

## 2. Rapid renewable expansion

MNRE's cumulative physical-progress table reports, as of **30 June 2026**, approximately:

- **162.15 GW solar power**;
- **57.44 GW wind power**;
- **236.52 GW renewable capacity excluding large hydro**;
- **288.59 GW total renewable capacity including large hydro**.

The scale of existing deployment means the research question is no longer simply where resource potential exists. India increasingly needs integrated intelligence on demand, grid readiness, storage, land, climate exposure, equity and system value.

**Official source**

- MNRE Physical Achievements, 30 June 2026: https://mnre.gov.in/en/physical-progress/

## 3. Power-system planning, storage and transmission

CEA continues to use the National Electricity Plan as the statutory generation-planning framework and publishes resource-adequacy and transmission-planning material. Government reporting in 2026 described a National Electricity Plan pathway of roughly **874 GW installed capacity by 2031–32**, a non-fossil share above 67%, and storage expansion up to **300 GWh**. CEA is also planning transmission for integration of more than 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030 and has listed planning work for more than 900 GW by 2035–36.

This strengthens the rationale for URJA-2047's grid, storage, resource-adequacy and 2047 demand modules.

## 4. Bhuvan/ISRO as a national geospatial infrastructure

Bhuvan provides a strong India-first geospatial foundation for URJA-2047. Current Bhuvan services include thematic data, Open Data Archive access, climate/environment applications, OGC web services, administrative and hydrologic base layers, and a dedicated Night Time Light portal/atlas. Bhuvan also exposes LULC and other thematic products through downloadable datasets and WMS/WMTS services. ISRO explicitly identifies solar, wind and wave-energy assessment as Earth-observation applications.

The proposed programme will use Bhuvan/NRSC data wherever licensing and spatial resolution fit the objective, while retaining globally harmonised inputs when needed for reproducible national screening and cross-validation.

## 5. Statistical backbone

MoSPI released **Energy Statistics India 2026** on 30 March 2026. The publication integrates reserves, capacity, production, consumption and import/export information for coal, lignite, petroleum, natural gas, renewable energy and electricity, and includes energy-balance and sustainable-energy indicators. URJA-2047 should use this publication as a core national statistical reference alongside PPAC, CEA, MNRE and state/utility data.

**Official source**

- PIB release for Energy Statistics India 2026: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246819

## Implication for URJA-2047

The evidence supports a proposal centred on **energy security + sustainability + climate resilience + equity**. India's transition problem is not a shortage of targets or resource maps. It is the need for a spatial decision system that connects current use, adequate service need, renewable opportunity, storage/grid feasibility, import-risk reduction and development priorities at national and local scales.
